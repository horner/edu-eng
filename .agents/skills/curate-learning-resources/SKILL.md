# Curate learning resources

Turn an area of expertise into a small, useful set of resources that helps a student learn and apply it. Preserve the project's emphasis on collaborative engineering, communication, experimentation, and root-cause analysis alongside individual technical knowledge.

Use the current repository's README and resource conventions when present. Search and recommend resources for the requested topic and level; update the local catalog when requested. Treat external maps and repositories as references unless the user specifically requests contributing to them.

## Discover and inspect

- Establish the capability the learner should develop, prerequisite knowledge, and intended depth. Reuse existing context rather than asking for information already supplied.
- Search YouTube or its indexed results for the specific concept and relevant synonyms. Include highly ranked or widely appreciated candidates, subject experts, university lectures, and smaller specialist channels.
- Use popularity and visible feedback to discover candidates. Record counts or rankings only when actually available, with the observation date. Compare relevance to the topic and audience rather than assuming the largest channel teaches every topic best.
- Search interactive platforms such as Brilliant, authoritative course sites, open textbooks, and suitable practice resources. Preserve the user's preferred sources while considering better topic-specific alternatives.
- Inspect the resource itself where possible: video, transcript, lesson, reading, or exercise. State the basis of evaluation. Metadata alone verifies identity, not an explanation's accuracy, entertainment value, or teaching quality.

## Evaluate accuracy and engagement separately

Accuracy and relevance are conditions for recommendation. Check central claims, equations, assumptions, terminology, and scope against an appropriate authoritative textbook, original research, official documentation, or expert course material. Identify consequential simplifications, dated material, and gaps. Mark an unverified candidate for further review rather than presenting it as validated.

Value entertainment and engagement explicitly: visual reasoning, demonstrations, storytelling, curiosity, humor, pacing, and clarity can all contribute. Explain which qualities were observed. A lively introduction and a sustained expert lecture can serve different purposes; avoid judging every resource by the same length or style. Keep engagement judgments distinct from correctness so an entertaining presentation cannot compensate for a substantive error.

Use review labels that reflect the evidence, such as metadata verified, transcript reviewed, or subject-expert reviewed. Do not claim faculty approval or complete video viewing without evidence.

## Link to learning, practice, and application

- Prefer the specific lesson, chapter, video, or exercise over a provider homepage. Verify titles, authors, destinations, and topic coverage. Use timestamps or section anchors when verified and helpful; never guess lesson identifiers or timestamps.
- For Brilliant and similar platforms, check whether the intended learner can reach the assigned content under the relevant account or subscription. Record any sign-in, payment, sequencing, or device requirements observed. An accessible course landing page does not establish access to every lesson.
- Link to the provider's original material. Prefer a stable public course page when an internal lesson URL is session-specific or cannot be verified. Record that limitation clearly.
- Seek a coherent learning sequence: an explanation to watch, something to read or explore, and an activity that applies the concept. Where appropriate, include a team application involving decomposition, interfaces, design review, explanation, experimentation, or root-cause analysis.
- Identify the assessment or observable performance that would demonstrate the target capability. Distinguish proposed activities and assessments from exercises actually supplied by the provider, and distinguish completion tracking from demonstrated competence.

## Record and maintain recommendations

Follow an existing schema or format. Otherwise use a concise Markdown table or topic entry recording:

- Capability, level, and prerequisites.
- Resource title, creator, provider, format, and verified URL.
- Assigned segment, chapter, or lesson and duration when known.
- Why it fits, with separate accuracy evidence and engagement rationale.
- Access conditions, review status, and date checked.
- Practice, assessment, or project connection, including any remaining coverage gap.

Prefer a primary recommendation and purposeful alternatives over an undifferentiated list. A source may be valuable as an introduction, rigorous reference, interactive practice, or advanced extension without covering the entire topic.

When updating a catalog, preserve useful existing selections, explain consequential replacements, and check for broken local links and duplicates. Recheck external access and changed content when revisiting a topic. Schedule ongoing maintenance only when the user requests it.
