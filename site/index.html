Engineering education

Working backward from the ideal graduate.

What if students graduated with four years of experience actually doing engineering? Start with the capabilities graduates need, then design the projects, learning, and evidence that develop them.

Read the proposal Review the implementation plan

Planning stage. The four-year curriculum and interactive prototype are still to be implemented. This site introduces a proposal for discussion with Indiana Tech.

Practice engineering, every semester.

Multidisciplinary teams would own real engineering challenges, supported by faculty mentors and strong learning resources. Fundamentals, individual assessment, and collaborative projects would develop together.

Learn and demonstrate

Connect mathematics, science, and engineering to explanations, practice, experiments, and individual evidence of mastery.

Build as a team

Decompose problems, agree on interfaces, rotate leadership, communicate clearly, and integrate working contributions.

Fail fast, learn fast

Test assumptions early, investigate root causes, ask the Five Whys, and use retrospectives to improve the next attempt.

One curriculum. Two ways to explore it.

The planned prototype will connect eight semesters of biomedical study to competencies, prerequisites, resources, and assessment evidence. A Knowledge Navigator-style browse view and a roadmap-style graph will present the same readable YAML source.

The application will be a static site with no login. Progress and notes will stay in the visitor’s browser, with manual export and import. Indiana Tech branding will be the default, with configurable profiles for other universities.

Help shape the proposal.

Faculty, students, practicing engineers, and employers can review the assumptions, curriculum progression, resource choices, collaboration practices, and ABET crosswalk planned in the repository.
